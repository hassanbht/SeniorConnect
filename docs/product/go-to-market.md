# Go-to-Market

## 1. The only question that matters at launch

> How does the 30th senior hear about this?

Not the first (you know them) and not the 3000th (that is a different problem). The 30th.
If you cannot answer that, do not start Phase 1.

## 2. Launch geography: one Gemeinde, not one Bundesland

Pick **one municipality of 5 000–20 000 people**, ideally where you have a personal
connection. A community platform's value scales with local density, not with total users.
1 000 users spread across Austria is worthless. 200 users in one Gemeinde is a product.

Selection criteria:

```
[ ] 5 000 – 20 000 inhabitants
[ ] Has an active Seniorenbund / Pensionistenverband chapter
[ ] Has a Pfarre with an existing Besuchsdienst
[ ] The Gemeinde has a digitalisation budget line
[ ] You can physically be there monthly
[ ] Someone will introduce you to the Bürgermeister
```

## 3. The three-channel funnel

### Channel A — The daughter (primary, 60 % of seniors)

Target Sabine (45–60), not Maria (78). She is on Facebook, she reads the
Bezirksblatt, she searches „Hilfe für Eltern Pflege organisieren".

```
Message:  „Sie können nicht jeden Tag vorbeischauen. Jemand anderes schon."
Channels: local Facebook groups · Bezirksblatt · Elternvereine ·
          local employer HR newsletters (they have many employees with old parents)
Landing:  a one-page web page that explains it in 60 seconds and offers
          „Konto für meine Mutter/meinen Vater einrichten"
```

### Channel B — Institutional endorsement (trust)

A senior in Austria will not install an app because of an advertisement. They will if the
Pfarrer, the Gemeinde or the Seniorenbund chair mentions it.

```
Gemeindezeitung  a half-page article, not an ad
Pfarrblatt       one paragraph after the Sunday announcements
Seniorenbund     a 20-minute presentation at their monthly meeting
Gemeindeamt      printed Zugangskarten at the counter
Apotheke, Arzt   a small poster with a QR code
```

Prepare a **printed A5 Zugangskarte**: large type, a QR code, a 6-digit code, and one
sentence. This physical object is the single highest-converting asset for this audience.

### Channel C — The anchor organization (density)

One NGO branch or the Seniorenbund chapter brings 20–40 already-trusted volunteers and
20–40 already-known seniors on day one. This solves cold start.

What you offer them, free, for the pilot:

```
· their own organization space with their branding
· volunteer hour tracking that replaces an Excel file
· a monthly impact PDF they can hand to their funder
· your personal support for three months
```

What you ask for:

```
· one named coordinator as your product partner, 2 hours a month
· permission to reference them (after the pilot)
· their honest feedback, including the parts you will not want to hear
```

## 4. The 90-day pilot plan

```
Weeks -4..0   Anchor partner signed. Legal gate passed. Referral directory curated.
              50 Zugangskarten printed. Coordinator trained.
Week   1      20 volunteers onboarded and verified. 5 groups created by seniors.
Weeks  2-4    First 20 seniors, mostly via family. First help requests.
              You attend every failure personally.
Weeks  5-8    Widen: Pfarrblatt + Gemeindezeitung. Target 40 seniors.
              Weekly 30-minute call with the coordinator.
Weeks  9-12   Measure. Write the impact report. Interview 10 users about what to remove.
Week  13      Decision: expand to a second Gemeinde, or fix and re-run.
```

## 5. Metrics that matter (and the ones that do not)

**Track:**

```
Activation      % of created senior accounts that complete one activity in 14 days
Density         active users per 1 000 inhabitants in the pilot Gemeinde
Fulfilment      % of help requests matched within 24 h
Repeat          % of seniors with 2+ completed activities
Retention       % of volunteers still active at day 60
No-show         % of assignments that end in a no-show   ← your headline improvement
Coordinator     hours per week spent coordinating, before vs. after
Time-to-help    median hours from request to assignment
```

**Do not track or report:** downloads, registrations, page views, "community members".
Every failed social platform reported those numbers right up until it closed.

## 6. Pricing (from Phase 2) — **revised after discovery**

| Product | Price shape | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Community (individuals) | free forever | never charge a senior or a volunteer |
| Gemeinde / Funder dashboard | **flat annual fee, small floor** | not per seat — see below |
| Organization | **flat annual fee per branch**, banded by active volunteers | not per seat |
| Impact & reporting module | included in the organization fee | it is the reason they buy |
| Corporate | per employee / year | Phase 8 |

**The per-seat model is dead on arrival.** F9 records it as an explicit red line:

> "…an unreasonable, recurring licence cost for small municipalities."

A per-seat price also punishes exactly the behaviour you want — adding more
coordinators and more volunteers. Band by active volunteers (BR-ROSTER-03: *active*,
not roster size) so the price tracks the value delivered, and set the bottom band low
enough that a 6 000-person Gemeinde can approve it without a debate.

Anchor pilot partners get a permanent founder discount. Say so in writing; it costs you
nothing and it closes the first three deals.

## 7. The buying process — *from F9*

Two very different paths, both annual, both gated on GDPR.

### NGO

```
Coordinator (champion, no budget)
      ↓  she must be able to say "this saves me two weeks in December"
Abteilungsleiter + Finanzverantwortliche
      ↓
Annual budget line + a GDPR compliance review
```

### Gemeinde

```
Sozialreferent:in (champion)
      ↓
Sozialausschuss
      ↓
Gemeinderat vote
      ↓
Council budget calendar — miss it and you wait a year
```

### What this means practically

1. **Ask for the budget calendar in the first conversation.** "When is next year's
   budget decided, and who signs?" Timing beats features here. A brilliant demo in
   February and a budget locked in November is a twelve-month delay.
2. **Build the champion's internal case for her.** She has to defend this to a
   finance officer. Give her a one-page document with her own numbers in it — hours
   recorded, time saved, active volunteers — generated by the product itself.
3. **The GDPR compliance pack is a sales artefact, not a legal chore.** Requested in
   both processes. Prepare it as a deliverable (Phase 7):
   ```
   Verarbeitungsverzeichnis · data-flow diagram · DPA template ·
   EU hosting attestation · deletion and retention policy ·
   pen-test summary · subprocessor list
   ```
4. **Answer the insurance question in writing before the first pilot meeting** (F4).
   The Gemeinde has already been burned by an unclear coverage dispute. Walking in
   without an answer is walking into the one topic they remember.
5. **The two red lines to never cross:**
   - complicated signup for a senior or a volunteer (NGO)
   - any personally identifying data in the funder dashboard (Gemeinde)

## 8. The pitch, in three sentences

> Soziale Organisationen koordinieren Nachbarschaftshilfe heute per Telefon, Zettel und
> Excel. Die Jahresauswertung kostet zwei Wochen im Dezember. SeniorConnect macht daraus einen
> nachvollziehbaren Prozess: wer ist tatsächlich aktiv, wer ist wofür freigegeben, was ist
> passiert, wie viele Stunden waren es — und die Auswertung dauert einen Nachmittag.
> Im Pilotprojekt in [Gemeinde] haben wir 412 Freiwilligenstunden dokumentiert, die vorher
> niemand gezählt hat, und die No-Show-Rate von 23 % auf 6 % gesenkt.

Fill in the real numbers after the pilot. Until then, do not pitch — build and interview.
